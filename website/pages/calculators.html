FINANCIAL ADVISORY INSIGHTS

4 Critical Factors Every Student Borrower Must Know

Don't sign a loan sanction letter without understanding how amortization, moratorium interest, and tax rebates work together.

1. Moratorium Simple Interest

During your degree, you don't pay EMIs, but lenders charge simple interest on disbursed funds. When your course ends, this interest is added to your principal, forming the base on which future EMI is calculated.

2. Section 80E Tax Savings

Unlike home loans which cap interest deductions at ₹2 Lakhs, Section 80E allows 100% deduction on all education loan interest with no upper cap for 8 consecutive years, reducing net interest burden by up to 30%.

3. Refinancing (Takeover) Post-Grad

If you take an unsecured loan from an NBFC at 12%–14% initially, you can refinance your outstanding balance to a PSU bank at 8.5%–9.5% once you start working, saving lakhs in interest over your remaining tenure.

4. Collateral vs Non-Collateral

Secured loans against residential property or fixed deposits offer the lowest rates (8.5%–10%) and higher limits (up to ₹2 Cr+). Non-collateral loans disburse faster (5–7 days) with limits up to ₹75 Lakhs.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Education Loan Calculator FAQs

How does an education loan EMI calculator differ from a standard personal loan calculator?

Unlike personal or home loans where monthly repayments begin immediately, education loans incorporate a mandatory moratorium period (course duration + 6 to 12 months grace period). During this study phase, simple interest accumulates. Our calculator accounts for this accumulated interest, delivering true repayment figures rather than artificially low estimates.

Can I claim tax deductions on my education loan repayment under Section 80E?

Yes! Under Section 80E of the Indian Income Tax Act, 100% of the interest paid on an education loan borrowed for higher studies (for self, spouse, or children) is fully deductible from taxable income with no upper monetary cap for up to 8 continuous assessment years under the Old Tax Regime.

What is an education loan takeover or refinancing?

An education loan takeover allows you to transfer your existing loan balance from a higher-interest lender (such as an NBFC charging 12%–14%) to a leading PSU or private bank offering lower rates (8.5%–9.5%) after you complete your degree or secure employment. This cuts your monthly EMI and saves lakhs in interest.

Do I have to pay EMIs during the study moratorium period?

No mandatory EMI is required while you are studying. However, some institutional lenders offer a 0.5% to 1% concession on interest rates if you choose to pay simple interest (partial EMI) during the study tenure.

Are the figures in these calculators final and binding on banks?

The figures are calibrated benchmark estimates based on official underwriting formulas across our 15+ partner lenders. Final sanction limits, applicable interest spreads, and loan conditions are determined by the respective bank or NBFC following credit assessment and KYC documentation.
